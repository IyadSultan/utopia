1 · How it works

One Rust binary, one Postgres. Documents go in; a graph of entities and facts comes out, with every fact carrying two clocks and a pointer to the sentence it came from.

The ontology is a contract

Extraction can only write types and relations that the ontology already defines. A term the ontology lacks becomes a counted proposal, adopted by evidence or by a person. "No type" and "no relation" are honest states, not fallback classes. The ontology itself is loaded from OWL/RDFS files (schema.org, PROV-O, your own).

Two clocks, nothing overwritten

World time When was this true? valid_from / valid_to, with precision from year to second, and an explicit "ended, date unknown". Diagnoses are states, procedures are events, blood group is eternal.
Record time When did we come to believe it? recorded_at / invalidated_at. A correction inserts a new row that supersedes the old one. Every read can be rewound: what was true on date X, as we knew it on date Y.

Rules and governance

People write rules over attribute values ("ANC below 500 and temperature above 38.3 → febrile neutropenia"). Conclusions keep their premises as a proof and retire automatically when a premise is superseded. Every human and agent decision goes to an append-only ledger the application database role cannot edit. An agent that adjudicates duplicates reads its own precedents first, and is switched off automatically if two of its merges are reverted in a week.

The one design decision that matters: codes are values, not classes. An ICD code lives on a condition.code fact as a FHIR-style {system, code, display, version}, retrieved into the prompt from a code table the pipeline can never add to. Loading 70,000 ICD codes as entity types would let the pipeline propose new diagnoses, which is the one thing a coding system forbids.

3 · What it takes to make a digital twin

The word is used loosely. A 2025 scoping review of 149 healthcare "digital twin" papers found that only 12% met the National Academies definition. Be precise about which rung you are on.

Digital modelBuilt once, updated by hand. No automatic data flow.
Digital shadowAutomatic flow from patient to model. A person acts on it. Records and reasons; does not predict.
Digital twinPersonalised, continuously updated, predicts, and feeds decisions back into care.

With the medical layer in place, one patient entity and everything reachable from it, read at a chosen world time and record time, is the shadow. Move the world clock back and a mastectomy becomes a future event and the febrile-neutropenia conclusion disappears. Move the record clock back and you see what the tumour board could have known. That part exists today.

Sources
  • Registry diagnoses (ICD-10), labs, chemo administrations, admissions: ingested as coded facts, no LLM
  • Pathology, radiology, progress notes, tumour-board minutes: extracted
  • Warehouse tables mounted read-only, queried at question time
The shadow
  • Bitemporal patient subgraph with evidence
  • Rules: febrile neutropenia, AKI stage, protocol eligibility, with proofs
  • Contradictions between pathology and clinic notes made visible
  • Chat and MCP for clinicians and agents; RDF / FHIR export for auditors
To become a twin
  • A predictive layer (mechanistic or ML) that reads the shadow and forecasts trajectory
  • A feedback path into care, with a clinician in the loop
  • Verification, validation and uncertainty quantification, prospectively
  • Calibration monitoring and recertification as the patient and the data change

Validation ladder for the paper

Claims escalate: descriptive → predictive → counterfactual → interventional → population. The shadow supports a descriptive claim: it faithfully records what the sources said, when it was true, when we learned it, and who decided what. Test it with extraction accuracy against two clinician annotators, a tumour-board replay "as of" the board date, and rule matches compared with existing SQL worklists. Everything above that rung needs a prospective study the shadow does not yet support.
